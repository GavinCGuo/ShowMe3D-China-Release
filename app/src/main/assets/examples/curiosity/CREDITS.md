# Curiosity assembly example sources

- Printable parts and build instructions: NASA Science, "Curiosity Rover
  (Detailed)", https://science.nasa.gov/3d-resources/curiosity-rover-detailed/
- Layered clean and Mars-dirty rover Blender sources: NASA/JPL-Caltech,
  "Curiosity Rover (MSL)", NASA 3D Resources,
  https://github.com/nasa/NASA-3D-Resources/tree/master/3D%20Models/Curiosity%20Rover%20%28MSL%29
- Mars panorama: NASA/JPL-Caltech/MSSS, "Curiosity Captures a 360-Degree View
  at 'Nevado Sajama'",
  https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia26696-curiosity-captures-a-360-degree-view-at-nevado-sajama/

The clean NASA Blender source is exported at its saved frame-180 showcase pose
into the complete rover and the body, suspension, six distinct wheels, mast,
and robotic-arm teaching parts. Evaluated world transforms are baked into one
shared assembly frame; pivot/shadow helper meshes are excluded while the real
shoulder-to-elbow link is retained. The final Mars page uses the project's previously validated NASA
complete rover GLB (`curiosity_complete.glb`) so its weathered PBR appearance is
preserved. For page 2, the source's rigid frame 1–180 node animation is converted
to one fully weighted bone per part and baked into the skinned
`curiosity_clean_animated.glb`; a short curled-pose hold precedes one complete
unfold. Blender export and geometry/render QA are reproducible via
`tools/export_nasa_curiosity_assets.py` and `tools/verify_curiosity_assets.py`.
The cylindrical Mars mosaic is extended into a 2:1
headset-friendly sphere with generated sky and near-ground; the photographed
terrain itself is unchanged. `martian_ground.glb` and `martian_rock.glb` are
procedural supporting geometry generated by this project.
